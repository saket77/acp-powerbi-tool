        Databook Client Portal: Product Brief
             Oct 26, 2026   · @Someone
        I want a small client portal, linked from the ACP website, where consultants publish a
        finished Interactive Databook (.pbix) themselves and clients view it after a secure sign-in
        with 2FA. It runs on Power BI Embedded ("embed for your customers") on one small
        Fabric F2 capacity paid by ACP. Everything after the upload is automated; I only act as
        support. First goal: a working demo at EUR 0 that convinces the ACP partners.


        The problem
        ACP builds an Interactive Databook (a Power BI report on one ACP template) for about
        200 client projects a year, and wants to sell it to clients as a product, like loyos bi.
              Sharing is manual today. Workspaces, permissions and uploads would all go through
              me. I am one person and cannot do that for 200 projects.
              Clients need a simple, secure view. They sign in with 2FA, see only their own
              companies, and install nothing.
              Why now: the template is stable, and the partners are open to a product if a demo
              shows it works.


        Who already does this
        The pattern is common: a branded portal where each client logs in and sees only their
        own Power BI reports, embedded with a token and row-level security. Look at these for
        the look and the flow; most are vendor pages, so treat their claims as marketing.

        How to embed Power BI in the portal
        The pattern is Microsoft's "embed for your customers" (App Owns Data): the backend
        talks to Power BI as the robot account and hands the browser a short-lived embed token;
        the viewer never signs in to Power BI.
        Once, at setup
           1. Register an app in Entra ID (this is the robot, a service principal) and store its secret
              in Key Vault.
           2. In the Power BI admin portal, allow service principals to use the Power BI APIs, limited
              to one security group that holds the robot.
           3. Have a Fabric capacity ready; new workspaces are assigned to it.
        Per project (backend, as the robot)

        The Client role filter is PATHCONTAINS ( USERPRINCIPALNAME (), Companies[Ges.] ) .

        Ground rules
              No real client data and no real Databook outside ACP's tenant. I provide a fake-data
              file only if the partners agree.
              Build everything as infrastructure as code (Bicep or azd ), so ACP IT can rebuild it in
              ACP's tenant without changes.
              Keep secrets in Key Vault, never in the browser or in code.
              Code and documents belong to ACP; we agree that in writing before production.
              EU region only (Germany West Central or West Europe).


        Test first, then build
        Four short spikes decide whether the design holds; please do them before the screens.
           1. RLS through the viewing pass. Pass two or three company names in the effective
              identity and check the Client role filters correctly, including names with spaces,
              umlauts and long lists.
           2. Upload by the robot account. Import a .pbix of realistic size into a new workspace
              with the service principal, and read the roles and the Template Version measure to
              run the checks.
           3. F2 is big enough. Open the heaviest Databook page (Cash Flow with all levels
              expanded) on F2 and watch memory and throttling. Fallback: F4 or F8.
           4. One robot or many. Check whether service principal profiles are needed for about
              200 workspaces, or whether one service principal is enough at first.
